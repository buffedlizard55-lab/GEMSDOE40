 ⚠ The portal error "Predicted values must be in range [0, 1]" — fixed, and here is exactly how 
 DrivenData rejects a submission whose values fall outside [0, 1] , and it also rejects
a raster whose nodata tag is a large negative sentinel such as
 -3.4028234663852886e+38 , because that sentinel is not a value in [0, 1] .
Two different failures produce the same message, and this repository has measured both. 
 Mechanism 1 — the float32 sentinel. training_features.tif uses
 -3.4028234663852886e+38 as nodata: 7,113,308 cells, of which 3,061 lie
 inside the submission footprint. Writing those through unchanged puts them outside
 [0, 1] . 
 Mechanism 2 — the NaN sentinel. A raster carrying nodata=NaN with NaN
cells outside the footprint is a legitimate competition format, but it is one validator change away
from the same rejection. Every primary download here is written all-finite , with
 nodata=None . 
 The fix, verified by re-reading the written bytes: every GeoTIFF offered on this site is
single-band float32, EPSG:32611, 100 m, 3730×3292, every one of the 12,279,160 cells
finite , min 0.0, max 1.0, zero cells outside [0, 1] , zero NaN , and
 no nodata tag . Each download ships a
 checks-*.json format receipt produced by re-opening the file from disk. 
 What this is 
 GEMSDOE32 is an auditable system for the DOE GEMS Prize (DrivenData #306, GeoDAWN / NW
Nevada): find geothermal-indicative faults that are not in the USGS / INGENIOUS
catalogue, and ship them as a legal GeoTIFF. It has three parts: an exact re-implementation of the
competition metric and its decision theory; a preregistered spatially-blocked holdout that scores
 emission rules under that metric; and a Bayesian-optimisation slot gate that decides when a
scarce weekly submission is worth spending. 
 Verified in this repository 
 the published metric formula, the published worked example, and the identity
 DTI = T/(0.2(T+S−M)+0.8|G|) (the suite runs in CI on every push: pytest tests -q ); 
 the credit bar k > 0.2·DTI that every emitted pixel must clear; 
 the byte identity (sha256) of every raster this site offers; 
 the official source links on the sources page. 
 Not claimed 
 no score in this repository is organizer-verified; the group's own numbers are labelled
 claim (4 of them, see leaderboard ); 
 the holdout's truth is the visible catalogue, so it cannot reward a genuinely new fault
(IR-32-PROXY-01); 
 the submitted file is a candidate, not a proven improvement. 
 The brief's question, answered 
 Why did the dotted H19-5 file score highest? Because the metric is a budget: every unit of
prediction mass that is not the best cover of a truth pixel costs 0.2, and one that is earns at most
1. Thinning a thick surface while keeping its geometry removes mass that was already covered — it
raises the credit per emitted pixel and moves the file to the point where the marginal pixel's
credit equals the break-even bar. The group measured that break-even empirically at
 0.0548  credit per dot; this repository derives the same number from the published
formula, 0.2·0.26 = 0.0520 . Two independent routes, one answer. 
 Can we beat 0.3262? Only by raising the
 credit density of the top of the ranking: at the same emitted mass the leader needs
≈25 % more mean credit per pixel than the group's best field delivers. No public catalogue can
supply that — the newest public compilation is already inside the given catalogue
(59065
px, of which all but one lie within 300 m of it). The path is a better detector plus the
two-round objective, and the plan is ranked here . 
 Two instruments, and the disagreement between them 
 The blocked holdout scores emission rules against the catalogue ; the model Monte Carlo
scores candidate files against a generative description of the hidden set. They do not agree
about every change — and that disagreement is a finding, not noise to shrug off:

 Mechanism. The holdout's truth is the catalogue, so it rewards covering the field surface
where the catalogue runs. The hidden set is scattered around the surface (the group's own
inference: a 1.85 px scale), so covering the surface is the wrong objective — the right one is to
cover the surface blurred by that scale, which is what the shipped file does. Same mass, same field,
same metric; only the objective changed.
