One frozen experiment.

Ranked by expected scientific value for DTI improvement, not invented score forecasts. H4 was implemented first in this frozen slate; H5–H7 were not tuned against its result. One important viability boundary

H6 needs verified altitude and datum information before projecting to the ground surface. The free official USGS GeoDAWN release lists flight-line CSVs and contractor reports. Metadata is obtainable; complete payload access and suitable datum information have not been verified. We therefore do not call that ground-projection variant viable.

What is novel here—and what isn’t

Euler inversion, contact offsets and rank reduction are established mathematics. The specific corrected dual-field construction is different from the inspected repository implementations, and its raster passes the dated all-prior audit. That is a bounded method/artifact claim, not a claim to invent Euler theory.

What happens next

  1. Register a new, feasible, independent whole-fault-system holdout protocol. Keep the old gate/results intact; do not use H4’s failure to lower a success threshold retroactively.
  2. Audit the native gravity bandwidth and survey drape datum. Synthetic planar success is necessary but not sufficient for a real depth interpretation.
  3. Test H5 on synthetic finite steps, then a locked geologic benchmark, before considering another competition slot.
