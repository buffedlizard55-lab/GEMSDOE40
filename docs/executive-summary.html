Scientific target

Faults—not vents, springs, or reservoirs.

The DOE GEMS Prize Challenge asks for geological faults that may indicate geothermal resources. A potential-field contact is not automatically a fault; a mapped fault is not automatically permeable; and neither establishes a viable reservoir. GEMSDOE40 uses Euler depth-deconvolution and multi-height persistence as source-location hypotheses, not as direct geothermal-site predictions.

The registered scientific ideas were: (1) magnetic–gravity depth consensus; (2) upward-continuation persistence in TMI Euler solutions; and (3) focal-mechanism-constrained orientation. H1 and strict H2 stopped at feasibility. H2-B, a separately registered rule emitting only natural positive KDE support, was evaluated and failed. H3 remains blocked pending measurement of official ComCat mechanism coverage.

Future upload guide

Only upload after the gate says eligible.

Current status: not eligible. The following steps are for a future candidate that has passed the frozen holdout, exact format, and uniqueness gates. They are not instructions to upload the H2-B file.
  1. Confirm the candidate page and validation report say ELIGIBLE FOR USER REVIEW, not HOLD.
  2. Read the dated report and confirm the candidate hash is the one linked beside the download.
  3. Download the single-band TIFF. Do not alter its pixels, nodata, or metadata after validation.
  4. Open the official DrivenData submissions page ↗, select the file, and enter only the name/note displayed for that promoted artifact.
  5. Before confirming, check that the portal accepts `[0,1]` values, then save the actual submission receipt. Do not infer that an upload succeeded from a local test.
  6. Record the resulting account-level score, date, and file identity separately. A leaderboard row does not attribute a score to a TIFF unless the organizer exposes a file-level link.

Why the range check matters

The portal error “Predicted values must be in range [0, 1]” is addressed by read-back validation: all in-footprint values must be finite and between 0 and 1, with the sample's outside-footprint nodata semantics preserved. This candidate passed that technical check; that alone does not make it scientifically suitable.
