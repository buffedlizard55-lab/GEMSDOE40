2. Why this emission

  1. The method is the brief's method: the ranking surface is a density of depth-labelled Euler solutions weighted so tight, shallow, mutually-consistent clusters outrank scattered or deep ones.
  2. The ring-prune is evidence-anchored. In the 19-field prior-geometry study: the highest-scoring file on record kept zero mass within 2 px of the catalogue; every prior with mass inside 2 px scores below every prior that avoided it at matched mass; Spearman(owner score, fraction inside 2 px) = −0.589; Spearman(owner score, emitted pixels) = −0.872. Shipped geometry: 0 % inside 2 px, median distance 21.2 px, 14.9 % inside 6 px, 85.1 % beyond 6 px.
  3. Most distinct field in the programme: maximum |Pearson| 0.0128 against all 19 prior rasters (h18-3a-x-complexity), maximum Jaccard 0.0093, zero near-duplicates.
  4. Best of the seven H4 surfaces on the frozen gate: ring-pruned 0.2860; raw KDE and every flank-gated variant ≤ 0.117 at their best budgets.

4. Rejected alternatives — and the instrument disagreement that rejected them

The emitter's own surrogate model preferred the flank-band family: euler_line_flank_gated (30,000 px, 4 px lattice) modelled 0.3410 and euler_line_flank_gated_corrob (25,000 px) 0.3370 — both above the 0.2824 modelled incumbent. The frozen gate rejects both (LM-cal 0.1168 / 0.0968; 0 of 4 folds; projected 0.028 / 0.008). Diagnosis:

  1. No corpus analogue. All 19 calibration priors spread mass across the footprint; the flank-band fields put 93–96 % of their dots inside 2–6 px of the catalogue. The two-parameter model is extrapolating; the gate computes a metric-shaped ratio per field and is not.
  2. The SGMC surrogate rewards catalogue-hugging; the real ladder punishes it. 12.8 % of the off-catalogue SGMC truth lies in the 2–6 px band, which is 3.9 % of the footprint — 3.3× enrichment from two agencies digitising the same structures. Across the corpus the direct SGMC metric is anti-correlated with published scores (Spearman −0.921).
  3. Guard added. run_h4.py now refuses to auto-select a surface with > 50 % of its mass within 6 px of the catalogue unless a human forces it with --surface, and every receipt records the distance-band fractions, the shipped geometry and the gate reference.

The reserve files were deleted after measurement; their receipts (032134Z, 032157Z) and the gate results are retained as the audit trail.

6. Independent byte audit

scripts/audit_submission.py re-reads the shipped bytes and re-checks: identical shape, transform, CRS and footprint to sample_submission.tif; single-band float32; every finite value in [0, 1]; no nodata sentinel (the cause of the portal error "Predicted values must be in range [0, 1]"); no positive value on a published-catalogue pixel; SHA-256 agreement with the receipt; uniqueness against all 19 priors. Both twins PASS; the script exits non-zero on any failure. The -zeros twin is the portal file (finite everywhere, values 0/1 only); the -nan twin follows the sample's NaN-outside convention with identical positive support.

7. What is not claimed

  • No organizer score. The leaderboard exposes no filenames or hashes; every (file, score) pairing here — including the 0.2778 reference — is an assumption. The projected leaderboard DTI (0.197 ± 0.021) is an affine projection of a proxy fitted to nine such assumptions (R² 0.913, RMSE 0.0107).
  • The modelled 0.1579 is not a score — it is a two-parameter regression output (RMSE 0.031, R² 0.807, LOO RMSE 0.0381) and, for this geometry, an extrapolation.
  • The SGMC mirror is a proxy (sha256 d569d553…700f). An earlier repository record used 26d142c4…b5c for the same filename; the conflict is unresolved because no USGS-side original is reachable from the build sandbox.
  • The pixel-exact mask convention is read from a DrivenData staff clarification, not the rules PDF. The shipped file does not depend on it, but the scoring model discussed above does.
