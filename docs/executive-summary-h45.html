01 / THE SUBMISSION FORM

Name and note, verbatim.

A — UNIQUE NAME

Submission title

gemsdoe40-h45-eulerdepthreadcluster-20261006-f28e5cff6826

Distinct from every name in the 44-site register and from all 343 cached prior artifacts. The trailing 12 hex characters are the SHA-256 of the exact bytes, so the name and the file cannot drift apart.

B — SHORT NOTE

Distinguishing note

“Euler deconvolution depth-clustering over the magnetic and gravity layers — a depth-labelled solution cloud weighted by shallowness, Euler-equation misfit, depth precision and local depth consistency, turned into a continuous field by weighted KDE. Emitted as ~300 m dots with the 200 m known-fault ring excluded. Different method and different pixels from every GEMSDOE1–44 submission (largest Jaccard 0.016).”

C — WHICH FILE

Upload the zeros variant

Use …-zeros.tif. Every cell is finite and inside [0, 1], which is what the form validator requires. The …-nan.tif twin is provided for research only; it carries NaN outside the footprint and can be rejected by the form.

A ZIP of the zeros variant is also published for convenience.

04 / THE HONEST ESTIMATE

Why this is a research release.

Calibration measurements ↗

The holdout is broken, and this session measured how.

Across the owner's own submissions, agreement with the public catalogue is monotonically anti-correlated with the reported public score: the 0.2600 submission has a catalogue lift of 2.18, the 0.2778 submission has 0.70. A spatially-blocked holdout scored against labels.tif ranks all four of them backwards (blocked DTI 0.032 / 0.021 / 0.010 / 0.002). Any instrument built on the public catalogue is therefore not merely weak — it points the wrong way.

Two effects are measurable and both are exploited here. First, mass within 200 m of a mapped fault scores zero: the ring-mass share is 0.116 / 0.074 / 0.030 / 0.000 across the 0.2600 → 0.2778 ladder, monotone in the wrong direction. Second, dots should be spaced ~300–600 m, not solid: the credit for a truth pixel is a maximum over the 300 m kernel, so neighbours do not add, and the repository's own ledger is an A/B test of exactly this (d2.8 → 0.2600 vs d1.5 → 0.2477).

A forward model fitted to those four scores — DTI = T / (0.2 M + 0.8 G) with G ≈ 5,667 truth pixels and credit-per-unit-mass 0.0890 — reproduces all four scores to within 2%. Its uncomfortable conclusion is that all four have the same placement efficiency; their score gaps come almost entirely from total mass and ring mass. H45 emits 43,038 dots at 400 m median spacing with zero ring mass, which puts it at or above the incumbent operating point provided its within-shell placement is no worse.

That last clause cannot be verified without spending a slot. Every proxy available is either anti-correlated or non-discriminating. So the estimate is: plausibly ≈ 0.28, with real upside if the Euler ordering helps, and no way to prove it in advance.

The Euler solver itself is verified against synthetic truth — tests/test_euler_h45.py, 10 tests passing, covering sign conventions, point-mass and line-mass depth recovery, the horizontal-line-of-dipoles index correction, along-strike pinning, finite-step edge position, SI=0 offset invariance and scale-free residuals. What is not verified is that the recovered contacts are faults rather than unfaulted lithologic boundaries, which is the standing caveat for Euler deconvolution and the reason the catalogue lift sits at 1.0.
