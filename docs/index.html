The brief’s question, answered

Why did 0.2778 score?

The metric is a budget. DTI = T / (0.2(T + S − M) + 0.8|G|). Every unit of prediction mass that is not the best cover of a hidden-truth pixel costs 0.2. H33-2-B2 is 37,654 dots, 0 on the public catalogue, ~300 m spacing matching the 300 m kernel, and a 2-pixel catalogue-flank prune of a surface that had already scored 0.2600–0.2708 live. Thinning a thick surface while keeping its geometry raises credit per emitted pixel. That is a budget result, not a new detector.

Can we beat 0.2778 / 0.3262?

Not by copying that geometry, and not by emitting a dense gradient map. Beating 0.3262 requires higher credit density on the hidden set. No public catalogue can supply that — the hidden set is faults the USGS/INGENIOUS map does not contain. Euler depth-clustering is a different physical observable (source location and depth of a contact, not an edge of the field). Whether those contacts coincide with the hidden expert traces is an empirical question this repository cannot answer without a live slot. We do not claim a score.

What was actually computed (verified, no hallucination)

  • Reid, Allsop, Granser, Millett & Somerton, Geophysics 55 (1990) 80–91 — 3-D Euler deconvolution, structural index 0 for a fault-like magnetic/gravity contact. Author PDF: reid-geophys.co.uk · DOI 10.1190/1.1442774.
  • Fields: official band rtp (reduced-to-pole magnetics) and band iso_grav_anom (isostatic gravity), windows 8 px and 12 px, stride 4 px.
  • 101,234 magnetic + 33,594 gravity depth-labelled solutions (median mag depth 192 m, grav 355–534 m).
  • Weighted KDE: shallowness × precision × local tightness × mutual depth-consistency × mag–grav concordance.
  • Public catalogue + 2 px dilation forced to 0 (the hidden test is off-catalogue).
  • Holdout (unmasked, same-count binary vs catalogue): Euler DTI 0.0258 vs |∇RTP| gradient baseline 0.0225, Δ +0.0032. Euler is not gradient thresholding.
